Founder Wrought-O-Meter

The draconian changes to CGT taxation under the proposed FY27 Budget stand poised to trigger a severe brain drain across the Australian startup ecosystem. By effectively abolishing the long-standing 50% CGT discount for founders and startup employees, Australia faces the highest effective tax rate on startup capital gains anywhere in the developed world. The likely outcome is fewer founders building in Australia, weaker employee incentives, more companies and talent moving offshore, and a significant reduction in long-term innovation and corresponding job loss.

What's changing

Announced under the banner of housing affordability but reaching far beyond, pending reforms target the 50% CGT discount on gains accrued after the change takes effect. Two transition mechanisms are under discussion:

  • Mark-to-market at 30 June 2027 - the unrealised gain from your cost base up to the company's value at 30 June 2027 accrues at that date under current rules (50% discount).
  • Time-based apportionment - the total gain at exit is split pro-rata by days held before and after 1 July 2027. The pre-change portion retains the 50% CGT discount.

In both cases any uplift from 30 June 2027 to your eventual exit is taxed at your full marginal rate with no discount. Enter your scenario below to see the cash tax impact of each approach.

Assumptions
  • Top marginal tax rate: 47% (incl. 2% Medicare levy).
  • 50% CGT discount applies to gains arising before 1 July 2027 on assets held more than 12 months.
  • From 1 July 2027 the discount is replaced by cost base indexation (per BP2 2026-27, p.21). The proposal also includes a 30% minimum tax on net capital gains, which is not modelled here as it doesn't bind at a 47% marginal rate.
  • CPI assumed at 2.64% p.a. compounded - Australia's long-term average CPI 2010-2025. Indexation runs from 1 July 2027 to the exit date.
  • Under Mark-to-Market, indexation runs from the actual 30 June 2027 stake value (your input).
  • Under Time Apportionment, the asset is assumed to have grown at a constant linear rate over the holding period; indexation runs from the implied 30 June 2027 value derived from this assumption (cost base + total gain × pre-1 July 2027 share of days).
